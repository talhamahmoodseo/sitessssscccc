# Competitor analysis: uSERP (userp.io)

Studied 27 October 2026. Everything below comes from their live site: 28 pages
rendered in a browser, plus their sitemaps, page titles, headings, copy, calls
to action and page code. Where I give a number about them, it is from their own
pages. I could not see their traffic or backlink data (no Ahrefs/Semrush access
from here), so nothing below guesses at those.

---

## 1. Who they are, in one paragraph

uSERP is a Denver agency, 37 people, 575+ clients, 42 published case studies,
"#1 link building company on Clutch" five years running. Their clients are
mid-market and enterprise: monday.com, Robinhood, CrowdStrike, Freshworks,
Remote, Hotjar. They have moved from "link building agency" to "search
everywhere" (links + AI search + digital PR + fractional SEO). Their prices
say who they want:

| Service | Their price |
|---|---|
| Link building | from **$5,000/month**, 3-month start |
| AI search / GEO | from $10,000/month, 6-month start |
| Fractional head of SEO | from $6,500/month |
| Digital PR | $15,000–$100,000+ per campaign |
| Healthcare SEO | from $10,000/month, 12-month terms |
| Roofing SEO | from $3,000/month |
| A single link (implied by the pilot page) | **$600–$900** ("3 placements at our standard rates: $1,800 to $2,700") |

**What this means for us:** they are not really our competitor for the same
buyer. They want $10M+ companies. We sell a DR 40+ link for $150. The useful
part is *how* they sell, not *what* they sell.

## 2. The "3 free backlinks" offer: why it works

**Where it lives:** a thin bar at the very top of **every page**:
"NEW · Get 3 authority backlinks free. No catch, no contract. See if your brand
qualifies". It links to `/pilot/`, a full landing page with an application
form (Jotform).

**What the page does, step by step:**

1. **Headline that kills the fear:** "Get 3 authority backlinks. Free. No catch,
   no contract."
2. **Explains why it's free**, before you get suspicious. Three short cards:
   "A deck is a promise. A link is evidence." · "It costs us less than chasing
   you" (six weeks of sales calls cost more than three links) · "The only catch
   is the obvious one" (they think you'll want more).
3. **A comparison table:** the usual way to pick an agency (sample links from
   other clients, a contract before the first link, quality visible in month
   three) vs. the pilot (your own links, no contract, quality in 30 days).
4. **Tiny steps with numbers:** 2 min to apply · 24 hrs to hear back · 30 min
   call · 30 days to live links · $0.
5. **The same product, not a cheap sample:** "The same links our clients pay
   for." Same team, same vetting.
6. **An anchor price:** "Three placements at our standard rates: $1,800 to
   $2,700. Your cost: $0."
7. **Honesty box:** "What three links will not do: they will not move a
   competitive page on their own." This makes everything else believable.
8. **Hard qualification, published openly:** $10M+ revenue, 50+ employees,
   real commercial pages, and **the person who owns the SEO budget must join
   the call**. No agencies, no resellers, no grey niches, one pilot per company.
9. **Scarcity:** "3 brands a month. When the spots are taken, applications roll
   to the next month."
10. **Proof of what the full programme does:** six case-study cards with big
    numbers ($140K/month, 92% AI Mode visibility, 677K → 1.2M visits).
11. **The page is `noindex, nofollow`.** It's not meant to rank. It exists to
    convert people who are already on the site, and it keeps freebie hunters
    from finding it on Google.

**Why it works (the psychology):**

- **Risk reversal.** The biggest fear when hiring a link builder is paying for
  junk. "Free, keep the links" removes that fear completely.
- **Proof beats promises.** Every agency says its links are better. A live link
  on your own page is something you can open and check.
- **"Apply" sounds exclusive.** "See if your brand qualifies" turns a freebie
  into a filter. People want what they have to qualify for.
- **It's a sales filter in disguise.** The $10M revenue rule and "budget owner
  on the call" mean every free pilot is a sales meeting with a real buyer.
- **The maths works for them.** One client is worth $5,000+/month for 3+
  months, so $15,000+. Spending ~$1,000 of real cost to win that is cheap.
  That is the key: **the offer only works because a won client is worth
  15–20× the cost of the freebie.**

**Should we copy it? Yes, but our version has to fit our numbers.** Their
client is worth $15,000+. Our average order is $700–$1,300. Three free links
to every brand that applies would cost us more than most orders bring in. See
section 9 for an adapted offer that does make sense.

## 3. SEO strategy: how their site is built to rank

**Site size (from their sitemaps):**

| | uSERP | Editorial HQ |
|---|---|---|
| Main pages | 46 | 10 |
| Blog posts | **502** | 6 |
| Case studies | **42** (each its own page) | 3 (examples) |
| Team member pages | 45 | 0 |
| Webinar episodes | 14 | 0 |

**What they do well:**

1. **The homepage is their money page.** Title: "#1 Link Building Agency For
   Industry Leaders". H1: "The Link Building Agency Companies Hire When ROI is
   Priority #1". They **301-redirect `/link-building-services/` to the homepage**,
   so all their authority goes into one page for "link building agency" and
   "link building services". The keyword is in the title, the H1, the eyebrow
   and the FAQs ("FAQs About Our Link Building Services").
2. **One page per buying keyword.** "healthcare SEO agency", "healthcare GEO
   agency", "roofing SEO company", "roofing marketing agency", "digital PR
   agency", "AI SEO agency", "SaaS SEO agency", "enterprise SEO agency",
   "fractional SEO", "link building for lawyers". The keyword is the H1 every
   time: "The roofing SEO company that…".
3. **Very long, useful pages.** Industry pages run 5,000–6,400 words. That isn't
   filler: each one has market data, a calculator, a full service breakdown,
   pricing, timelines, "who this is for and who it is not", and FAQs.
4. **Linkable assets that earn links on their own.**
   - *State of Backlinks 2027*: their own survey of 116 SEO leaders, 20 charts,
     "free to reuse with credit". Other blogs cite it and link to it.
   - *87 Link Building Statistics*: a stats roundup. Writers looking for a
     number link to it.
   - *Should You Outsource Link Building?*: a decision-stage article for people
     about to hire.
   All three sit in the main menu under "Resources".
5. **Bottom-of-funnel blog posts.** "how to find a reliable link building
   agency", "blogger outreach services", "professional back link building
   services", even "buy backlinks". They write for people about to buy.
6. **Constant updating.** 54 posts were updated or published in October 2026
   alone (sitemap dates). Old posts get refreshed, which keeps them ranking.
7. **Third-party lists.** "Ranked #1 by Clutch, Growth Ramp, Single Grain,
   Codeless, SEO For Growth, and Respona." These "best link building agency"
   lists rank on Google and get quoted by AI answers. (Worth knowing: their own
   case studies call Codeless their "sister agency", so not every list is
   independent.)
8. **Founder authority.** Both co-founders are shown as "Forbes 30 Under 30",
   and the CEO as an Entrepreneur Leadership Network advisor. Their big report
   carries his name, title and an "updated" date.

**Where they are sloppy (and we are already better):**

- No meta description on About, Results, Contact (`/start`), Awards and Case
  Studies.
- The roofing *marketing* page uses the **Digital PR page's meta description**
  (a copy-paste mistake).
- Very little schema: only `Organization` on the homepage and `AboutPage` on
  About. No `Service`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList` or `Article` schema on
  service pages, industry pages or reports. We have schema on every page.
- The main menu links to `/link-building-services/`, which redirects. Every
  menu click costs a redirect.
- The older "link building for lawyers" page has **nine H1 tags**.
- Their numbers don't always match: the About page and homepage say "800+ SEOs
  surveyed", but the report itself says 116.

The lesson: a big, trusted site can carry these mistakes. A new domain can't.
Our clean technical setup is a real advantage. Keep it that way.

## 4. Industry pages: how they target healthcare, roofing and others

The roofing SEO page (6,441 words) is their best template. In order:

1. **Hero with the buyer's own maths:** "One month of SEO vs. one roof": a $2–5K
   retainer, $228 per paid lead, $9,609 per roof replacement → "Break-even:
   1 job". The buyer instantly sees the deal makes sense.
2. **Industry badges** (Clutch "Top SEO company · Roofing · 2026").
3. **The market size:** "Americans search for a roofer 4.1 million times a
   month", with a chart and sources.
4. **Rented vs. owned:** paid leads disappear when you stop paying; rankings
   stay. Written in the roofer's language: storm chasers, yard signs, door
   knocking.
5. **A working ROI calculator**, pre-filled with industry benchmarks.
6. **Every service explained in roofing terms:** map pack, city pages, cost
   guides, insurance claim content, `RoofingContractor` schema, local news and
   supplier links.
7. **"Six situations roofing owners bring us, and what to verify before
   spending":** objections answered before the call.
8. **A timeline:** "Map pack moves in 60 to 90 days. Organic compounds from
   month four."
9. **The price:** "Roofing SEO services from $3,000 per month".
10. **Who it's for, and who it isn't.**
11. A relevant case study, the team, FAQs, then the CTA.

The **healthcare** pages add one thing that's very smart: **compliance**.
"The objection that stalls healthcare SEO in compliance review, answered up
front", plus medical review built into the process. They name the client's
biggest internal blocker and solve it on the page.

**Why this works:** a roofer doesn't want "an SEO agency". They want someone
who already understands roofing. Every number, objection and example on the
page proves that.

**The warning sign:** their "industries" menu also lists SaaS, B2B, Ecommerce,
Trades, Staffing, Finance and Insurance, but most of these have **no page**.
Only healthcare and roofing get the deep treatment, and the old lawyer page is
thin. They build depth where they have proof, not a page for every niche.

## 5. Case studies and proof

- **42 case studies, each on its own page**, with a big headline result
  ("Ranking for 6.6K+ New Keywords and Boosting DR by +45 in 8 Months"),
  client location, company size, a "results at a glance" row, then the story:
  the client → the challenge → the solution in numbered steps → the results.
- **Real names and real logos:** monday.com, Nav, Remote, Hotjar, Preply.
- **A results page you can filter by outcome:** traffic, rankings, link
  building, revenue/ROI, Domain Rating, AI visibility.
- **Six video testimonials** from named people with job titles.
- **Every number links to its study:** "Organic traffic growth from our
  published case studies. Every bar links back to a study you can read."
  Their homepage bar chart (Track-POD 601%, StuDocu 500%, …) works like this.
- **Fine print that builds trust:** "Periods differ by engagement; see each
  study for dates." / "Visibility varies… not a promised outcome."
- **Business outcomes, not link counts:** "$140K a month in pipeline revenue",
  "8.6x ROI", "outranked NerdWallet". Even their link-heavy studies lead with
  revenue or rankings.

**Our gap:** our three case studies and the review wall are examples. This is
the single biggest trust gap between us and them, and only your real portfolio
can close it.

## 7. Their positioning gaps: where we can win

1. **They've left small and mid-sized buyers behind.** A $5,000/month minimum
   and a pilot for $10M+ companies only. Startups, small SaaS companies,
   e-commerce stores and local businesses that want quality links have nowhere
   to go at uSERP. That's exactly who we serve at $110–$499 per link.
2. **They've walked away from agencies.** The pilot excludes "agencies,
   resellers, and anyone applying on behalf of a client", and their
   `/white-label-link-building/` page now redirects to an old page. **Agencies
   are the best repeat buyers in link building.** We already offer 100% white
   label. This is our clearest opening.
3. **Their price per link is hidden behind a call.** Ours is public and
   instant. For a buyer who wants five links, not a "search everywhere
   programme", we are much easier to buy from.
4. **They've spread wide** (AI search, PR, fractional, roofing PPC, web design).
   We can be the specialist: "we only do links, and we're very good at it".

## 9. What we should do, in order

### Now (no portfolio needed)

1. **Make the homepage clearly target "link building agency".** Keep "Rank on
   Google. Get cited by AI." as the big line, but add "Link building agency"
   as the eyebrow above it, change the title to something like "Link Building
   Agency for Brands & Agencies | Editorial HQ", and name the FAQ section
   "Link building FAQs". *(Small code change.)*
2. **Fix lead capture.** Our contact form opens the visitor's email app; many
   people give up there. Switch to a form that sends directly (a free
   Tally/Jotform embed, or a WordPress form) and set your real Calendly link in
   the booking popup. *(I need your Calendly link.)*
3. **Turn the contact page into a "what happens next" page**, like their
   `/start`: "Talha reviews your site before the call", "you talk to the
   founder, not a sales rep", "we reply within 24 hours", "if we're not the
   right fit, we'll tell you". Add your photo and LinkedIn. Your 1,300 LinkedIn
   followers are proof that you're a real person; use that.
4. **Add a founder note to the About page**, signed by you, about why you
   started: a link builder who got tired of agencies selling spreadsheets of
   DR. Short, personal, true.
5. **Run our own version of the pilot offer**, adapted to our numbers.
   Suggestion (you decide the details):
   - **"Free proof link for agencies. See if you qualify."** One free DR 40+
     placement for agencies and SEO teams who resell or buy links every month.
     Five spots a month, by application, one per company.
   - **Why agencies:** uSERP excludes them, and an agency that likes one link
     can order 10–50 a month for its clients. A good agency is worth far more
     to us than one brand order, which is what makes a free link worth giving.
   - **Same page structure as theirs:** why it's free → comparison table → 4
     tiny steps → "the same link our clients pay for" → anchor price ("normally
     $150, your cost $0") → "what one link won't do" → published criteria →
     scarcity → application form. Set it to `noindex` like they do.
   - Show it as a **thin bar at the top of every page**.
   - Keep the **$129 trial** for everyone else (brands and agencies that don't
     qualify). That covers both kinds of buyer.
   - **Before we build it, I need two numbers from you:** what a DR 40+
     placement costs you, and roughly how many links a typical agency client
     orders a month. Those decide whether one free link (or two) pays for itself.
6. **Get on review platforms.** Create profiles on **Clutch**, **GoodFirms**
   and **Trustpilot**, and ask 5–10 past happy clients for a review. Clutch
   reviews are interview-verified, which is why uSERP leans on them so hard.
   Reviews from any past work count, as long as it was real link building
   work for that client.
7. **Upload the latest theme** (still pending): the live site still shows the
   old $497 pilot pricing.

### When your portfolio arrives

8. **Real case studies**, using the structure we already have, plus what uSERP
   does well: client type, location, size, one big result in the headline, a
   "results at a glance" row, then client → challenge → what we did → results.
   **Every number links to its source.** Even 3–5 real ones beat 42 vague ones.
9. **A "placement portfolio" page**: a list of real live placements by niche
   (publication, DR, niche, live URL, masked where clients need privacy).
   uSERP doesn't have this. For a link builder, a list of real links you can
   click is the strongest proof there is.
10. **Industry pages, but only 2–3, where we have real proof.** Based on our
    services, the likely ones are: *link building for SaaS*, *link building for
    e-commerce* and *white-label link building for agencies* (that one first,
    given section 7). Each page gets the uSERP structure, built around **links**
    (we're the link specialist, not a full-service SEO agency):
    - the buyer's own maths in the hero;
    - the kinds of sites that matter in that industry, with real example
      placements;
    - our 7-point check adapted to that niche (e.g. strict YMYL rules for
      health and finance);
    - "situations we see and what to check before you buy";
    - pricing, "who it's for / not for", a matching case study, FAQs.

    No thin pages for niches we can't prove.

### Ongoing (content engine)

11. **One linkable asset per quarter, built on data only we have.** After
    10,000+ placements, you know things nobody else publishes:
    - **"Guest Post Price Index 2027"**: what publishers actually charge, by DR
      band and niche. Journalists and SEO blogs will cite it.
    - **"What we learned from vetting X sites"**: how many fail, and why
      (traffic padding, link-selling footprints, deindexed pages), using real
      rejected-site examples like the three already on the link building page.
    - **"Link building statistics 2027"**: a stats roundup like theirs, which
      ranks and earns links for years.
12. **Bottom-of-funnel articles** for people about to buy: "how much do guest
    posts cost", "how to check a guest post site before you pay", "PBN vs real
    site: how to tell", "white-label link building: what to ask a vendor",
    "best link building services for small businesses". 4–8 a month is
    realistic for a small team.
13. **Refresh old posts every few months.** uSERP updated 54 posts in a single
    month. Updated dates and fresh numbers keep pages ranking.
14. **Get onto "best link building agency" lists**, through outreach (you
    already know how) and guest posts by you. These lists rank on Google and
    feed AI answers. Your LinkedIn posts can feed the same topics.

### What not to copy

- **The enterprise positioning and prices.** That market isn't ours, and
  "$5,000/month minimum" would scare off our best buyers.
- **Claims we can't back.** "#1 agency", "575+ clients", "800+ SEOs" only work
  because they have (most of) the data. Every number on our site stays
  verified. That's how we beat them on credibility.
- **Their mistakes:** missing meta descriptions, copy-pasted descriptions,
  redirects in the menu, numbers that don't match.
- **500 blog posts.** Volume isn't the lesson. Write for buyers and publish
  real data.

## 10. The short version

uSERP wins on **proof** (42 real case studies, Clutch, videos, real logos),
**risk reversal** (a free pilot with honest limits), **buyer-specific pages**
(roofing and healthcare written in the buyer's own language and maths) and
**content that earns links** (their own survey and stats pages). Their site
has technical mistakes, and they've walked away from small buyers and agencies.

Our plan: fix the homepage keyword and lead capture now, put you (the founder)
front and centre, run a free proof link aimed at agencies (their blind spot),
get real reviews, then build real case studies, a placement portfolio and 2–3
industry pages from your portfolio. And publish data only a link builder with
10,000+ placements has.
