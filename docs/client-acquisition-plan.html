1 · The big picture

Each channel does one job

Upwork

Catch demand

People already looking to hire a link builder today. Fastest money, most competition.
LinkedIn

Build attention

People who don't know they need you yet. Slower, but brings bigger, repeat clients and agencies.

Win trust

Where LinkedIn people go to check you out. Case studies, prices and the audit form close the deal.

↓

Free link audit→ Trial: 1 link, $250→ Package: 3, 5 or 10 links→ Monthly retainer

All three channels push to the same ladder. Nobody buys 10 links from a stranger, but many people say yes to a free audit, and a good audit sells the trial.

Where I'd push back

The website is not a daily channel. A new site takes 6 to 12 months to bring clients from Google on its own. Right now its job is to convert the people LinkedIn sends there. So you spend 2 minutes a day on it (checking the form) and about 2 hours a week, mostly done by your team member. That's what keeps this plan at two hours a day instead of four.

Upwork

4 · Catch demand

Fewer proposals, sent faster, written about them

On Upwork the first 1 to 2 hours after a job goes live decide most hires. Speed and a first line about their website beat long proposals every time.

Profile setup (one time)

  • Title: "Link Building & Digital PR | Authority Backlinks That Move Rankings"
  • Overview, first 2 lines (all that shows in search): the problem and your result, not "Hi, I'm Talha".
  • Portfolio: your SmileTutor and CryptoDesk Featured images as the first two items.
  • Project Catalog: 3 fixed offers that match your website: trial link $250, 3 links $750, 5 links $1,250.
  • Specialized profile for "Link Building" only. Don't mix in content writing or web design.
  • Same photo as LinkedIn, so people recognise you.

Job filters (save these searches)

  • Keywords: link building, backlinks, guest posting, digital PR, off-page SEO, white label link building, outreach
  • Payment verified
  • Client has spent $1,000+ and hire rate 50%+
  • Fewer than 15 proposals
  • Posted in the last 24 hours (best: last hour)
  • Skip: "$5 per link", "need 100 links", "DA 50+ for $20". Those clients never pay $250.

The proposal workflow (8 to 10 minutes each)

  1. Open their website (2 min). Find one real thing: a keyword they're on page 2 for, or a competitor beating them.
    You
  2. Write the first 2 lines about them, using what you found. This is the only part they see before clicking.
    You
  3. Add one matching proof: SmileTutor for local/service businesses, CryptoDesk for finance and hard niches.
    You
  4. Give a 3-step plan and ask one question. The question gets replies.
    You
  5. Follow up once after 2 days if they viewed it but didn't reply.
    You
Proposal template
Hi [Name],

I checked [their site]. You're on page 2 for "[keyword]", and the 3 sites above you all have links from [type of sites] that you don't have yet.

That's fixable with links from real sites in your niche. No PBNs, no link farms.

A recent example: I got a tuition agency to #1 on Google for "tuition" in Singapore, with +169% organic traffic. Case study is attached.

How I'd start with you:
1. A quick link gap check against your top 3 competitors
2. A shortlist of sites for your approval before anything is published
3. Placements, with a live report of every link

Quick question: which page matters most to you right now?

5 · Build attention

Post 3 times, comment every day, start real conversations

Your profile is done: banner, About, Featured and Experience. Now the daily work is getting the right people to see it. At your stage, comments bring more profile views than your own posts.

Posting: 3 a week

  • Mon, proof: a client result with a screenshot.
  • Wed, lesson: something you learned doing the work (site checks, outreach, pricing).
  • Fri, builder: an AI tool or automation you made, or your journey.
  • Every post has a story, proof or a number.
  • One in three ends with "DM me 'AUDIT'".
  • Carousels for proof and lessons. They already do best for you.

Daily outreach: 5 people

  • Who: SaaS founders, marketing heads, SEO agency owners, e-commerce owners.
  • Connect with no pitch. A short personal note or no note.
  • Like or comment on 1 of their posts before you ever DM.
  • Offer the audit only when there's a reason: they posted about SEO, traffic or a competitor.
  • Never send a pitch as the first message after they accept.
Reply when someone DMs "AUDIT"
Hey [Name], happy to do this.

Send me 3 things:
1. Your website
2. The main competitor you want to beat
3. The country you're targeting

You'll get a short video within 48 hours showing the links they have and you don't, and which ones are worth going after first. No call needed.
Opening a conversation (after they accepted and you've engaged)
Hey [Name], liked your post on [topic], especially the part about [detail].

Quick one: are you doing any link building for [their site] right now, or is it all content for the moment?

7 · The engine behind all three

The free audit workflow

The audit is your main offer on every channel, so it needs a set process. Your team does the heavy part, and you add the expert voice.

  1. Request comes in (LinkedIn DM, website form, or an Upwork client asking). Reply with the 3 questions.
    You · 2 min
  2. Run the link gap: their site vs the competitor in Ahrefs or Semrush. List 10 to 15 linking sites the competitor has and they don't, and mark the realistic ones.
    Team · 45 min
  3. Record a 4 to 5 minute screen video (Loom or similar) walking through the gap and the 3 easiest wins.
    You · 10 min
  4. Send it within 48 hours, with one line: "If you want, I can start with one trial link for $250 so you can see the quality first."
    You · 2 min
  5. Follow up after 2 days: "Did you get a chance to watch it?" Then once more after 5 days. Then stop.
    You · 1 min
  6. Log it in your tracker: name, channel, date, outcome.
    Team · 1 min

11 · Rules that stop burnout

Keep it small enough to keep doing

Time-box everything. When the block ends, stop, even mid-scroll.
Never zero, never extra. Busy day = 20 minutes. Good day = 2 hours. No 5-hour days.
Batch the writing. All 3 posts on Sunday, with AI for the first draft and you for the voice.
One day fully off. Saturday: only reply to paying clients.
Hand off the repeat work. If a task happens every week and doesn't need your voice, it's your team's.
Judge the month, not the day. One bad week means nothing. Four bad weeks means change one thing.
