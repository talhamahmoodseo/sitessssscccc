The most important decision in this plan

Make SEO agencies your main LinkedIn target, not brands. A brand buys 5 links and leaves. An agency has 10 to 30 clients who all need links, every month, and can't build them in-house. One agency on white-label is worth 5 brand clients, and they find you on LinkedIn, not on Google. Brands still come in through Upwork and your website.

2 · Upwork

Win the top 20% of buyers, not the price shoppers

Link building on Upwork is crowded with $30 guest post sellers. You won't beat them on price, and you shouldn't try. Most of your time goes to spotting the buyers who care about quality, and showing them proof faster than anyone else.

The one thing that wins link building jobs: a site sample

Every link building buyer has the same fear: "Will the sites be junk?" Most proposals answer it with words. You answer it with evidence. You have your own website lists, so in every proposal you include 5 real sites from their niche, with traffic, DR and country, in a small table or a view-only Google Sheet. Don't share prices or contact details, just enough to prove the quality is real.

Why this works

It answers the fear before they ask. It shows you have inventory, not just outreach promises. And it takes your team member 5 minutes per niche, because you can reuse the same sheet for every SaaS, finance or health job.

Rules that protect your account

  • Invites first. Reply to every invite within the hour. Win rate on invites is far higher than on cold proposals.
  • You can't follow up on a proposal. Upwork lets you message only after the client replies, so the first message has to do all the work.
  • Boost only high-value jobs (agency, monthly, $500+). Don't boost small jobs.
  • Keep Upwork clients on Upwork. Moving them off breaks the terms of service.
  • Proposals come from you. Your team member prepares the shortlist and site samples, and you send.
  • Ask for the review the day you deliver, while they're happy.

3 · LinkedIn

At 1,400 followers, conversations bring clients, not posts

Your posts get 100 to 260 views. That's fine for building trust, but too small to bring buyers on its own. So in month 1, about 70% of your LinkedIn time goes to starting conversations with the right people, and 30% to posts. Your posts and profile do their job when those people click through to check you out.

Who to find (free LinkedIn search)

  • Agencies, your main target: titles "Founder", "CEO", "Head of SEO" at companies with "SEO" or "digital marketing agency" in the name. 2 to 50 employees. UK, US, Australia, UAE.
  • SaaS: "Head of Marketing", "Growth", "SEO Lead" at software companies with 10 to 200 employees.
  • Warm signals, do these first: people who viewed your profile, commented on your posts, or comment on other link building posts.

The value DM, which is where most people go wrong

  • Don't send "Hi, I offer link building services". Everyone sends that and everyone ignores it.
  • Send one specific, useful observation from 3 minutes in Ahrefs or Semrush: a lost link, a competitor gap, or a page that's close to page 1.
  • Offer the full audit only after they reply.
  • For agencies, the hook is capacity: "Who builds links for your clients?"
Value DM to a brand / SaaS (after you've engaged once)
Hey [Name], I had a quick look at [site]. Your [page] is at position [8–15] for "[keyword]". [Competitor] sits above you mainly because of links from [2–3 example sites].

That's one of the easier gaps to close. Want me to send you a short video with the full list?
DM to an SEO agency owner

I ask because I run link building white-label for agencies: niche-relevant sites, you approve every one, reports under your brand. Happy to do one free test placement for one of your clients so you can judge the quality.

1. Your website

Posts that bring DMs (not just likes)

  • Proof breakdowns with a real screenshot: what you did, how many links, what changed. These bring the most "how much?" DMs.
  • "I rejected this site" posts: show a high-DR site with spam keywords or a dead graph, and why you said no. Agencies love these because it's exactly their client problem.
  • Builder posts: your AI tools and automations. This makes you different from every other link builder and gets shares.
  • Comments on bigger SEO accounts, every day, with a real point from your work. On your account size, this is your biggest source of profile views.

Make it convert now, rank later

Now (convert LinkedIn traffic)

  • Track the source. Every link you share adds ?utm_source=linkedin. Then GA4 shows which channel brings the form fills.
  • Reply to form leads within 2 hours. A slow reply loses more leads than a weak page does.
  • Link case studies everywhere: in DMs, in your Featured section, in proposals (on LinkedIn only, not Upwork).

Later (rank on Google)

  • Build money pages before blog posts: "white label link building", "link building for SaaS", "link building for [niche]". These searches come from buyers.
  • Build links to your own site, 2 to 4 good ones a month. If you rank yourself, that's your best case study.
  • Turn your best LinkedIn posts into blog posts once a month. It's zero new writing.

5 · Audit to client

The audit is your sales call

Every channel ends here, so this is where you win or lose the client. A good audit makes the buyer think: "he already knows what to do, I just need to say yes."

+ offer 3 or 5 links"]
  H --> I["Package / retainer"]

Where audits usually fail

The audit is too long, too technical, or ends without a next step. Keep the video to 4 minutes, show only 3 wins, and always end with one clear, small offer. Don't send a 20-page PDF.
