## 3. Headline

LinkedIn shows about 45 characters on mobile before cutting off, so the
promise has to come first.

**Recommended:**
> I get brands ranked on Google & cited by AI | Link building, digital PR and AI brand mentions for SaaS, agencies and growing brands

The first 43 characters, "I get brands ranked on Google & cited by AI", are the whole promise.
The rest tells desktop readers how, and who it's for.

Other options:
- "Links from real publications, not link farms | I help SaaS and brands rank on Google and get named in AI answers"
- "Helping SaaS & brands rank on Google + in AI | Link building · Digital PR · Brand mentions | CapCut, SmileTutor, CryptoDesk"

## 4. About section

Structure (no "passionate professional with 10 years of experience"):
1. **Hook (first 2 lines, before "see more"):** a problem your buyers
   feel. For example: "Most backlinks people buy never get read by
   anyone. Google noticed. So did ChatGPT."
2. **The story:** how you saw this in client work, and what you do
   differently (hand-checked sites, real publications, a 7-point check).
3. **Proof, with numbers:** SmileTutor #1 for "tuition" in Singapore
   (+169% organic traffic). CryptoDesk #1 in Dubai in 5 months, 35+ press
   releases including Bitcoin.com. 18 buyer's-guide placements for a
   Defender launch.
4. **What you do:** link building, digital PR, brand mentions for AI search,
   expert quotes. One line each.
5. **Who it's for:** SaaS founders, agencies (white label), brands.
6. **Next step:** "DM me 'AUDIT' and I'll send you a free link audit
   within 48 hours", plus the booking link.

We write the full text together in this step. It stays under 2,600
characters (LinkedIn's limit) and uses short paragraphs that work on a phone.

## 5. Featured section (what people click and buy)

Three cards, in this order:
1. **The offer:** "Free link audit: see what's holding your rankings
   back". It links to your Calendly (or a short PDF explaining the audit).
2. **Best case study:** SmileTutor, #1 on Google for "tuition", posted as a
   LinkedIn document (PDF carousel) so people read it without leaving
   LinkedIn.
3. **Pricing:** "Links from $250. No contract." It points to a post or PDF with
   the packages (the website link can be added once you're ready).

## 6. Experience + Services

- **Experience:** one current entry. Title: "Link Building & Digital PR",
  company Editorial HQ. Write 3–4 lines of what changed for clients
  (numbers), not a list of duties. Attach the case study PDF.
- **Services page** (Profile → Add section → Services): link building,
  digital PR, brand mentions, HARO & expert quotes. This adds a "Request
  services" button and puts you in LinkedIn's service search.

## 7. Jargon sweep

Remove anywhere on the profile: first-class, innovative, leading, best-in-class,
results-driven, passionate, guru, ninja, turbocharge, synergy, "expert" as
a self-label, "10+ years of experience" as an opener. Replace each with a
fact or a number.

## 9. Posting system (weekly)

Lara's rule: **every post has a story, proof or a number.**

- **3 posts a week:** 2 expertise posts (what you know: site vetting, AI
  citations, outreach) + 1 proof post (a client result). Rotate clients so
  it's never the same name twice in a row (as in the profiles guide).
- **Every post ends with one next step:** "DM me AUDIT", or a question.
- **15–20 minutes a day of comments** on posts by SaaS founders, agency
  owners and SEO people you want as clients. Add a real thought, not "great
  post".
- **Proof bank (verified, safe to use):**
  - SmileTutor: #1 for "tuition", +169% organic traffic, 617 keywords in
    the top 3
  - CryptoDesk: #1 in Dubai in 5 months, 100+ placements, 35+ press
    releases, Bitcoin.com feature
