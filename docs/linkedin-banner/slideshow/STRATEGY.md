# LinkedIn slideshow banner: research and strategy

## 1. How the feature works (checked)

- LinkedIn calls it a **slideshow cover image**. It's available to
  **Premium Business, Sales Navigator and Recruiter** subscribers. Free
  accounts can upload one cover image but not a slideshow.
  ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7145577))
- **Up to five images.** They rotate automatically "every few seconds"
  (LinkedIn doesn't publish the exact interval).
  ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-revolving-slideshow-option-for-profile-banner-images/734691/),
  [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/linkedin-introduces-dynamic-cover-images-for-premium-subscribers-to-turn-profile-banners-into-slideshows))
- **Size:** 1584 × 396 px (4:1) per image, JPG or PNG.
- **Setting it up:** Profile → camera icon on the intro card → **Create
  slideshow** → upload → reorder with the arrows → **Apply**.
- **What sits on top of it:** your profile photo covers the bottom-left
  corner. In the slideshow, LinkedIn also adds a **dots indicator in a dark
  pill at the bottom-right** (visible in the reference screenshot). Both
  corners have to stay free of anything important.
- **The first image matters most.** It's what loads first, and it's the one
  people see if they leave quickly. It must work on its own (and is the
  single banner if the account isn't on Premium Business).

## 2. What the reference banners do well

All three belong to one profile. Each slide has **one job**, and they
look like one brand because **only the message and the picture change**:

| Kept the same on every slide | Changes per slide |
|---|---|
| Charcoal background with fine grain | The message |
| A small wayfinding line top-left | The visual (person, diagram, real photos) |
| Category label in a pill, same place | Which accent words |
| Two-line headline, same size and place | |
| One short subline | |
| Two accent colours, used only in the headline | |

- **Story across slides:** what he does → how he does it → the outcome.
- **Small word count:** a label, a 4–7 word headline and a 10–16 word
  subline. You can read each slide in about two seconds, before it moves on.
- **Clear hierarchy:** headline first, subline second, label third.
  Nothing competes with the headline.
- **The text block starts about one third of the way in**, clear of the
  profile photo, and is vertically centred. Visuals sit on the right.

## 3. What we'll do better

1. **Proof.** The references show no clients and no numbers. Our third
   slide shows real results with real screenshots (SmileTutor's Semrush
   growth and its #1 Google result), and the first slide shows three client
   logos.
2. **A real call to action.** "Explore my work ↓" is passive. Ours is the
   action we want: "Free link audit · DM me 'AUDIT'". It's the same on
   every slide, so it's seen 3 times per visit.
3. **Readable on phones.** On a phone the banner is shown at about a
   quarter of its size. Their sublines become hard to read, so ours are
   at least 24 px (about 6 px on a phone) and every slide has fewer words.
4. **Corners kept clear.** Nothing important under the profile photo or the
   slideshow dots.
5. **Clean visuals.** Their third slide puts text over a busy photo. Ours
   keeps the evidence in neat cards beside the text, not behind it.

## 4. Brand choices

- **Colour:** charcoal (`#17161B`, warm and slightly violet), white text,
  and your purple in two tones: **violet `#9D86FF`** and **lilac
  `#D3C9FF`**. One hue, two tones, like the references' two accents but
  calmer. Purple comes from your logo and your current banner. No other
  colours.
- **Type:** Plus Jakarta Sans, your website font. Bold headline, medium
  subline, small uppercase label.
- **Photos:** your two real cutouts (arms crossed; head and shoulders), so
  slide 1 and slide 2 don't repeat the same picture.

## 5. Content rules (from the owner, round 2)

The design was right; the first wording read like website or CV copy. A
banner's only job is a strong first impression and clear positioning.

- One strong message per slide, said the way an SEO link building expert
  would say it. The model is the owner's own line: "Forget low-quality
  backlinks. Build authority links that move your rankings."
- **No** client names or logos, no project results, no internal process
  (e.g. "7-point check"), no lists of services. That belongs in the
  profile, Featured section and website.
- Every line must make sense to someone who knows nothing about us.
