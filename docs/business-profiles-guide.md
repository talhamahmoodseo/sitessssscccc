# Editorial HQ: profiles that sell (v2)

Version 2 of the profiles playbook. Version 1 covered *which fields to fill
in*. This one covers *how a profile wins a client*: what a visitor has to see
in five seconds, where your proof goes, how to show testimonials, and the
direct pitch on every platform. Every line below is ready to copy.

---

## 0. Review of version 1: what changes and why

| Version 1 | The problem | Version 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptions said what we do | Every agency says that. Nobody hires on a description | **Lead with a result**: "We took SmileTutor to #1 on Google for 'tuition'" beats "We build quality links" |
| No proof on the profiles | A profile without proof looks like every freelancer's profile | A **proof bank** (section 2), placed on every platform: bio line, highlights, featured posts, portfolio |
| No call to action | Visitors liked the profile and left | A **direct pitch** on every profile: the free 3-link pilot for established brands, the $200 trial for everyone else |
| Company page first | People buy from people. Your personal LinkedIn has 1,300 followers, the company page has 0 | **Your personal LinkedIn is the main shop window.** The company page backs it up |
| Testimonials missing | Clients approved quotes for the website, but none were on the profiles | Testimonials as **Instagram highlights, LinkedIn Featured posts, pinned posts and Clutch reviews** |
| Instagram treated like a bio | An empty grid kills trust faster than no account | **A 9-post launch grid** before you share the profile anywhere |

What stays from version 1: the same name everywhere (`Editorial HQ`), the
handle `editorialhq`, `team@editorialhq.com`, UTM links, 2FA, no freelancing
platforms on brand profiles, and real numbers only.

## 1. The 5-second test (use it on every profile)

A buyer gives your profile about five seconds. In that time they must answer
four questions. If any answer is missing, fix the profile before you post.

1. **What do you do?** → "Link building & digital PR agency"
2. **Does it work?** → one hard result: "#1 on Google for 'tuition'"
3. **Can I trust you?** → client logos, a testimonial, a real face (you)
4. **What do I do next?** → one clear action: "3 free backlinks if you qualify"

Rule of thumb: **one result, one face, one action** above the fold of every
profile.

## 8. Trustpilot

Its job: stars in Google results for "Editorial HQ reviews".

- Sign up at business.trustpilot.com with `team@editorialhq.com`, domain `editorialhq.com`, category **Marketing Agency**.
- Description: use the first paragraph of the Clutch description.
- **Invite every client when you deliver their report**, using Trustpilot's own invite link, so reviews show as *Verified*. Make it a fixed step in your delivery checklist.
- Reply to every review within 48 hours, two lines, specific:
  > "Thanks Rum, getting SmileTutor to #1 for 'tuition' was a team effort. Glad the reports made it easy to follow."
- Never pay for reviews, never only invite the happy clients, never write your own. Trustpilot flags all three.
