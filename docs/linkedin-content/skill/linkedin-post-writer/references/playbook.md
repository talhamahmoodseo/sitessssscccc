# The Lara Acosta LinkedIn playbook: what this skill applies

This summarises the playbook PDF (`lara-acosta-playbook.pdf`) and Lara's video "6 rules". When in doubt, the PDF wins.

## What wins on LinkedIn in 2026
- Strategy beats format. Text, photos, screenshots, carousels and video all win or lose on the same psychology.
- **The anti-AI shift.** Readers are tired of polished, copy-paste "AI slop". Raw, conversational, imperfect posts with lived proof win.
- **Lifetime creator vs viral creator.** Viral creators chase trends, and if you cover their name, nobody knows who wrote the post. Lifetime creators put their own stories, photos and frameworks into every topic, until readers recognise them without seeing the name.
- Repetition builds memory. Tie at least 3 posts a week to the core experience that makes you qualified. Repeat your biggest proof about once a month. Readers don't remember yesterday's post.

## Positioning (3 questions)
1. What do you want to be hired for?
2. What are you specifically great at?
3. Who is actively paying for that?

Weak positioning is a category label ("marketing consultant"). Strong positioning is a specific outcome plus a mechanism.

## The 4-3-2-1 system
- **4 posts a week:**
  - Tuesday Educate: PAS or a full framework, "How I…" language.
  - Wednesday Document: build in public, real numbers, never a "what I did today" diary.
  - Thursday Storytell: SLAY or POV, universal human struggles.
  - Weekend Sell: halo effect, end-to-end breakdown, customer-eyes transformation, or a lead magnet.
- **3 pillars:** Educate captures attention, Storytell builds trust, Sell converts.
- **2 audiences:** the ideal client (can this person help me?) and the ideal follower (relates, amplifies, refers).
- **1 goal:** move people from LinkedIn ("rented land", where a post reaches only 2–3% of followers) to "owned land" (DMs, email list, waitlist).

## The three depth tiers
| Tier | Audience | Subjects | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broad | Ideal followers, beginners, adjacent markets | Mindset, self-doubt, imposter syndrome, starting from zero, rags to riches, unfair cultural or personal advantages, work ethic | Reach, humanise, new followers daily |
| Narrow | Industry peers, decision-makers | Industry shifts, hiring and team, leadership lessons, operational systems, trend analysis | Nurture, show mastery, peer credibility |
| Niche | High-intent buyers | Exact mechanisms, client teardowns, ROI numbers, specific playbooks | Pre-qualify buyers, trigger DMs |

## Frameworks
**SLAY**
- **Story:** 2–4 lines, a specific factual moment with a time frame. Example: "In the last 28 days I made $27,000 from an offer I never posted about."
- **Lesson:** the bridge to value. Without it, the story is just bragging.
- **Actionable:** 3–5 concrete steps, usable within 10 minutes.
- **You question:** turn the post back to the reader so they comment. Use "you" more than "I".

**PAS**
- **Problem:** an urgent threat or shift in lines 1–2. Example: "SEO is changing, but not the way you think."
- **Agitate:** sharpen the cost, so the reader thinks "this is happening to me".
- **Solution:** a clear step-by-step plan, closing with a decisive command.

**POV**
- The hook is "POV: You…", under 8 words. It makes the reader the main character.
- Low-to-high arc: start where you were (low status, a struggle), compress time, acknowledge the sacrifice, end at your current aspiration. Never start high and brag.
- Use sentence rhythm (short, medium, long lines) and white space.
- Tie it to universal desires: wealth, health, relationships.
- Close with 2–3 short feel-good lines.
- Pair it with a candid photo of the real scene. One POV a week; the next post can teach the steps behind the story.

Template:
> POV: You [milestone in <8 words]. Not long ago, I was [low starting point]. Statistically, [odds or obstacle]. Over the last [time], everything changed, not by luck, but because I focused on [1 insight]: → shift 1 → shift 2 → shift 3. I've had [honest sacrifice], but I've never felt more alive. [2–3 short closing lines.] [Candid photo.]

## Hooks in 2026
- Short hooks (under 8 words) are still ideal for POV posts.
- Otherwise, a raw, conversational 2–3 line opening is fine, as long as those lines prove the time will pay off and show why you're qualified.
- Readers scan in an F-pattern, so the first 2–3 words of *every* line must hook the eye.

| Fails (AI slop) | Wins (authority) |
|---|---|
| "How to build a 7-figure business:" from someone who hasn't done it | "How I…" / "After helping 50+ brands…": advice anchored in lived proof |
| "We had a record month!" plus a stock graphic | An exact number plus a raw, unedited screenshot |
| A generic infographic, "5 tips to say no at work" | A candid photo plus a real personal line plus 3 lived tips |
| Copy-pasted captions from other platforms | Context-first framing: what happened, the numbers, then the lesson |

## Ideas: never start from a blank page
- Study outlier posts in or near the niche. Save the hook, the line-by-line structure and the angle, then rewrite with your own story, numbers and voice. Copy the skeleton, never the words.
- The algorithm rewards relevance, not originality.
- **Multiplication matrix:** one real experience becomes 4 posts:
  1. "How I" educational: "This is how I closed X for £60,000 at 27."
  2. Data or result story: "In 2019 I closed my first £60K deal after years of charging £1K."
  3. Tactical listicle: "How to land your first high-ticket client (the exact structure)."
  4. Vulnerability: "Imposter syndrome almost kept me charging £1,000 forever."
- **Signature series:** one core idea, one repeatable pattern and a distinct visual style, repeated until people recognise it.

## Distribution and comments
- LinkedIn first tests a post on 1st-degree connections, then spreads it by conversation speed. Comments and replies count far more than likes.
- **1+3 outbound (30–60 minutes a day, same time):** comment on 20 relevant accounts. Leave one real "mini-post" comment each (a hook plus a story, case, counterpoint or data point; never "Great post"), and reply to 3 other commenters on the same post.
  - Engage peers and buyer-adjacent accounts, not only mega-creators, who rarely reply.
  - Stay inside the buyer's world, so LinkedIn categorises your account correctly.
- **3+1 self-comments:** right after posting, leave 3 comments under your own post:
  1. a behind-the-scenes update or photo;
  2. a repurposed past insight;
  3. a secondary takeaway or question.

  Then reply to 100% of comments.
- **Halo effect:** reference respected people or companies you learned from ("I studied Clay's 10 sales funnels…"), and appear on podcasts or lives where buyers gather.

## Selling without being sleazy
- Use one of four methods on Sell days:
  - a halo-effect authority post;
  - an end-to-end breakdown;
  - a customer-eyes transformation story;
  - a lead magnet.
- Use one clear CTA. Never put a sales CTA after a deeply vulnerable story.
- Lead magnets: a value-overload pack, a secret-formula teaser, or (the highest converting) a free AI agent or custom GPT that does the task for the reader.
- Offer killers:
  - a vague offer: map before → after and name a 3–5 step path;
  - saying the same thing the same way until people stop noticing: find a new angle;
  - no real urgency, no cost of inaction, no relatable proof from someone like the buyer.

## The pre-publish gate (from the playbook)
1. **Hook and F-pattern:** the first 1–3 lines show the payoff, and every line opens with strong words. A POV hook is under 8 words.
2. **Authority:** it answers "how do I know this / why trust me?" with numbers, time frames or real anecdotes.
3. **Anti-brag:** the story moves low → high, and uses "you" more than "I".
4. **Visual:** a specific candid photo, screenshot or visual asset is named.
5. **Close:** a sharp You question, or a lead-magnet CTA on selling days (never after a vulnerable story), plus the 3 self-comments.
