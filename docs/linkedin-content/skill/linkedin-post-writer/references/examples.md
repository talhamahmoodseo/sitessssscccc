# Approved examples and rejected patterns

Talha said the writing style of these drafts "looks good". Match their voice, rhythm and plainness. Don't copy their topics word for word.

## Approved example 1: comeback (Document, SLAY)

```
I didn't post on LinkedIn for almost a year.

Not because I quit.
Because I was busy doing the work.

In that time, I helped an education business rank #1 on Google for a one-word keyword with 30,000+ searches a month.

The links I built were one part of it.
Their content and SEO did the rest.

But one thing surprised me:
It took about 70 links. Not 700.

That's the lesson I keep coming back to.
Most link building fails because people buy numbers, not sites.

High DR. Nice traffic graph. Looks great in a report.
Then you open the keywords and the site ranks for casino and "net worth" terms.

So before any link goes live, I check 3 things:
→ What does the site actually rank for?
→ Is the traffic steady, or a spike and a crash?
→ Would a real reader in my client's niche ever visit it?

Fail one, and the DR doesn't matter.

I'm back to share what's actually working in link building right now. The wins and the mistakes.

What's the first thing you check before buying a link?
```

## Approved example 2: one-number thinking (Educate, PAS-style story)

```
I turned down a DR 80 site recently.

On paper, it was perfect.
Established domain. Any client would be happy to see it in a report.

Then I opened the data.
Almost no organic traffic.
A graph that goes up and down every month.

DR 80. Nobody reading it.

Here's what most people don't know:
DR only measures the links pointing to a site.
It says nothing about readers, rankings, or whether Google still trusts it.

So I check DR last, not first:
→ Traffic: are real people landing there from Google?
→ Keywords: do they match the site's topic?
→ Graph: steady for 12 months, or spikes and crashes?
→ DR: only after it passes the first three.

A DR 35 site with real readers in your niche beats a DR 80 site with none. Every time.

Have you ever bought a "high DR" link that did nothing?
```
Note: this is an insider topic. Use it at most once a month. For a broad audience, open with the lesson anyone feels ("I turned down a 'perfect' deal in 30 seconds") and keep the DR details inside the post.

## Approved example 3: building with AI (Document, broad appeal)

```
I'm not a developer.
But I built a tool that does [X hours] of my team's work in [Y minutes].

The problem:
Every link building project starts with a Semrush export.
Thousands of rows. Spam keywords mixed in. Dead sites.
My team cleaned it by hand. Every. Single. Time.

So I opened Google Apps Script and explained the problem to AI, like I'd explain it to a junior.

It wrote code. It broke.
I pasted the error back. It broke again.
[Number] tries later, it worked.

Now we paste the export, click one button, and get a clean list of sites worth checking.

What I learned:
You don't need to code to build tools.
You need to understand your problem really well.

If you want to try it:
→ Pick one task you repeat every week
→ Write down every step like you're teaching an intern
→ Ask AI for a Google Apps Script that does exactly that
→ Test it on real data and paste every error back

What's one boring task you'd automate first?
```

## Talha's own posts that did well (2 years ago), for his natural voice
- He opens playfully ("I am gonna give you a KISS. Yes, you heard it right.").
- He uses short lines, arrow lists and a P.S. with a question.
- The weakness: generic tips with no story or proof. Keep the playful voice, add real experience.

## Rejected patterns (don't repeat)
| What was written | Why Talha rejected it | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Posts on "the link that disappears", "same site, three prices", "why I stopped pricing links by DR" | Only link builders read them. His clients aren't on LinkedIn to learn link building | Insider topics: max 1–2 a month, framed as a lesson anyone feels |
| Hooks like "If you can't name the editor, you don't own the link", "Selling links is easy…" | They pull back into link-builder talk | A line-1 hook must work for founders and agencies too |
| "Content without authority is a diary" and similar slogans | Clever, but nobody stops scrolling | Hooks need a real moment, number, tension or challenged belief |
| "jumping up and down like a heartbeat", "a DR 80 ghost town" | Sounds written by AI | No metaphors or similes. Say what happened |
| Naming a client or saying "I ranked them #1" | The client wouldn't like it, and content and SEO also played a part | No names, no identifying details, "I helped" |
| Content-calendar ideas that were generic or random | Not built from the playbook or from his situation | Every post: slot + tier + framework + one of his 3 advantages |
| Fixed package prices in LinkedIn posts | Pricing is discussed in chat on LinkedIn | Don't post prices unless Talha asks |
| Too much effort on layout and structure | He cares about the quality of the content | Spend the effort on the hook, the proof and the lesson |
