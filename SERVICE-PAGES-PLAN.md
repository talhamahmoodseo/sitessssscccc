# Service pages — content and structure plan

Written before any rebuilding, so sections exist for a reason rather than to
fill space.

## 1. What the references actually do

Measured from the live pages, not guessed.

| Page | Words | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dofollow.com/link-building-services | **1,131** | value → approach → trust → process → differentiation → CTA → FAQ → CTA |
| dofollow.com/visibility-plus | **898** | problem → gap → method → transformation → **named system** → vs-tools → tactics → CTA → FAQ |
| dofollow.com/case-studies/career-platform | **577** | outcome headline → 3 stat cards → project facts → chart → goal/solution/result → CTA → related |

Five things they get right:

1. **Short, not bloated.** ~900–1,100 words. Every section is a claim plus
   proof. There is no "what is link building" padding.
2. **A named system.** `AnswerOS™` turns a service into a product. It implies
   IP, and it gives the sales conversation a noun.
3. **Contrarian honesty.** *"Not Every Visibility Problem Needs Another
   Backlink."* Refusing the upsell buys more trust than claiming everything.
4. **FAQ is pure objection handling** — pricing, payment, can-I-see-the-sites,
   reporting, white label. Not SEO filler questions.
5. **Case studies lead with the number** in the H1 and repeat it in stat cards
   before any prose.

Where they are beatable — and where we win:

- Their site lists are **not** shown ("Can we see your list of sites?" is an FAQ
  they answer defensively). Our actual differentiator is the opposite: **every
  domain disclosed and approved before placement, roughly one in five rejected.**
  That is a stronger position and we should build the pages around it.
- Their proof is aggregate. We can show a **sample placement** and a real
  rejection.
- Their pages are generic across industries. Ours can speak to the specific
  buyer (agency, SaaS founder, SEO lead, e-commerce, local services) — we
  already have those personas.

## 2. The positioning spine

Everything hangs off one idea, which is already true of the business:

> **The Open Ledger** — you see every domain, every metric and every rejection
> before anything is placed, and the same ledger shows what each placement did
> afterwards.

*(Name is a proposal — happy to change it. The point is to have a noun for the
method, the way Dofollow has AnswerOS™.)*

This gives each page a spine: *here is the work, here is what we refused, here
is what it produced.*

## 3. Shared page architecture

Applied to all four service pages so the site feels like one product. Target
**1,400–1,700 words** — deeper than the references, still no filler.

| # | Section | Job | Serves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero: outcome headline + one-line promise + dual CTA | Match intent in 3 seconds | Conversion |
| 2 | Proof strip: 3 hard numbers | Credibility before claims | Conversion |
| 3 | The problem, named honestly | Agitate what they already suspect | SEO + trust |
| 4 | What you actually get | Remove ambiguity on deliverables | Conversion |
| 5 | **The Open Ledger** — our vetting standard, with a real rejection | The differentiator | Trust |
| 6 | Process, 5 steps with what *you* do vs what *we* do | Reduce perceived effort | Conversion |
| 7 | Worked example with real numbers + chart | Show, don't claim | Trust |
| 8 | Who this is for / **who it is not for** | Qualify, build trust by excluding | Trust |
| 9 | Pricing context + what changes the number | Pre-empt the #1 objection | Conversion |
| 10 | Objection FAQ, 7–9 questions | Close remaining doubt | SEO + conversion |
| 11 | Related services + case studies | Internal linking | SEO |
| 12 | Closing CTA with the low-friction option | Capture the not-ready-yet | Conversion |

**CTA rhythm:** hero → after the ledger (§5) → after the worked example (§7) →
closing (§12). Four, spaced so one is always within a screen of scroll, never
stacked back to back.

**Two CTA tiers throughout:** primary *Book a 15-minute call*, secondary *Get a
free visibility teardown* for people not ready to talk.

## 4. Per-page specifics

### 4.1 Link Building — `/services/link-building`

- **Primary intent:** commercial — "link building services", "buy backlinks",
  "link building agency". Buyer is comparing vendors and is suspicious.
- **Must understand:** these are editorial placements on sites with real
  traffic, not a marketplace reselling inventory.
- **Objections:** Are these PBNs? Can I see the sites first? What DR? Will it
  get me penalised? How long until it moves? What if a link is removed? Do I
  approve anchors?
- **Trust signals:** approval-first workflow, ~1-in-5 rejection rate, 96%
  indexation, sample placement, link-replacement guarantee.
- **Proof:** the B2B SaaS case (DR 35 → 65, 14 placements, +212% clicks).
- **Unique section:** *"What we reject and why"* — three real examples of sites
  that failed vetting (traffic bought, outbound profile dirty, no editorial
  standards). Nobody in this space shows that.

### 4.2 Digital PR — `/services/digital-pr`

- **Primary intent:** commercial-investigational — "digital PR agency", "digital
  PR vs link building". Buyer may not know which they need.
- **Must understand:** PR earns coverage that links follow; it is slower,
  higher-ceiling, and compounding.
- **Objections:** Is this guaranteed? How is it different from link building?
  What if journalists ignore it? Do I need a newsworthy story? Who writes it?
- **Trust signals:** named outlets with DR, a real pitch that landed, response
  rates.
- **Proof:** Gulf/trade press case (DR 74 top outlet, 21 placements, +88%
  revenue).
- **Unique section:** *"The pitch that worked"* — an actual subject line, angle
  and the resulting placement, annotated.
- **Cross-link:** explicit "Digital PR vs Link Building — which do you need?"
  comparison block. Captures a real search and links both pages.

### 4.3 Brand Mentions / AI Visibility — `/services/brand-mentions`

- **Primary intent:** emerging — "get cited by ChatGPT", "AI SEO", "LLM
  visibility". Buyer is curious and sceptical that anyone can influence this.
- **Must understand:** AI engines cite the same publications Google trusts;
  unlinked mentions count; this is measurable.
- **Objections:** Can you actually influence an LLM? How do you measure it? Is
  this just link building rebranded? Why not wait?
- **Trust signals:** before/after prompt screenshots across four engines,
  tracked citation share, honest limits.
- **Proof:** "from invisible to cited everywhere" case, 3/3 AI surfaces.
- **Unique section:** *"How we measure a citation"* — the actual method, prompt
  set, cadence, and what we cannot promise. The honesty is the differentiator.

### 4.4 HARO & Expert Quotes — `/services/haro-links`

- **Primary intent:** commercial — "HARO link building service", "expert quote
  placements".
- **Must understand:** this is a managed pipeline from journalist relationships,
  not spray-and-pray on a free platform.
- **Objections:** Isn't HARO dead? Who writes the quotes — is it my voice? How
  many per month? What if there is no link? Do I need to be available?
- **Trust signals:** sample approved quote, expertise-profile process, scoping
  on delivered placements not attempts.
- **Proof:** two DR 65+ quotes in month one.
- **Unique section:** *"Your expertise profile"* — show the intake that makes
  quotes sound like the client, which answers the real fear (generic quotes
  published under my name).

## 5. SEO structure

- One `<h1>` per page carrying the primary term naturally.
- `<h2>` per section using the question/phrase people actually search.
- FAQ marked up with `FAQPage` JSON-LD; service pages with `Service` schema.
- Internal links: every service page links to the other three, to its matching
  case study, and to pricing. Case studies link back to the service.
- Descriptive anchors ("read the B2B SaaS case study"), never "click here".
- Images: real alt text; charts rendered as inline SVG so they are crawlable
  and fast.
